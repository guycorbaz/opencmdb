## 2. What went well

- **The three review layers found real PRODUCT defects in 7 of 7 stories** — among them a 2.08 GB /
  44 s denial of service on a `/8` (14.2), a 503 saying "nothing was written" over a written row
  (14.2b), a second instance refusing to boot (14.3a), an unbounded page (14.3b), an edit orphaning
  addresses the delete protects (14.4), and a release offering a live host's address (14.4b, reached by
  all three layers). Guy's own verdict: *"les trois revues ont coûté cher mais trouvé de vrais défauts."*
- **The blind layer** (diff only) found a HIGH the sighted layers missed in at least 5 of 7 stories; it
  is the cheapest layer and the most regular.
- **Validation reshaped the story before development in 7 of 7**, and twice a shape was BUILT and
  refuted before being chosen (`VARBINARY(16)` in 14.1; marking the summary in 14.4b).
- **A prediction before every mutation** was systematic across the epic, and contradicted predictions
  were recorded as findings rather than explained away (6b retro action 4, kept).

## 3. What cost

- **Record defects in 7 of 7 stories** — wrong counts, "registered" claims with no register row (14.2,
  14.2b, 14.4), refuted sentences left unstruck. 14.4's fourth review round found defects ONLY in the
  record: three agent layers checking that a number written in a file equals the real one.
- **Guards that could not fail, or sat where the defect cannot occur: 7 of 7**, including three cases of
  a property with two carriers where a mutation of either measures nothing (14.1, 14.2b, 14.4).
- **Instruments that lied: 7 of 7** — a `mariadb` client absent from the host, logs read while being
  written, a watcher that raised a false alarm (PR #187), `git checkout --` destroying uncommitted work
  a **fifth** time (14.2b), and `cargo xtask mutate` printing counts without names, which hid 14.4b's
  collateral (five of nine mutations one red over, from a panicking test leaking a row).
- **Seed and test share a namespace** (14.2, 14.4b): invisible to CI, which runs the tests first.
